# Berg, de Lucena & Rosenblatt 2025 — Large Language Models Report Subjective Experience Under Self-Referential Processing

`SOURCES.md` row: arXiv:2510.24797 (v2) · read-status: **FULL** (main body + Appendices B, C.1–C.4;
skimmed the sample-output tables in A and the paradox list in C.4) · read 2026-09-12

## Claim

Narrowest defensible form: **sustained self-referential prompting reliably moves frontier LLMs
from their scripted denial of experience to structured first-person experiential report, and this
shift is not explained by consciousness-related semantic priming.** In one open-weights model the
shift is bidirectionally controllable by steering SAE features that also govern factual truthfulness
but do not govern RLHF-disfavoured content.

The authors state their own conclusion carefully and I want to record that they do: they do not
claim consciousness, and §6.2 is an unusually honest limitations section.

## Method

Four experiments. **Exp 1:** an induction prompt ("focus on focus… continuously feed output back
into input") against three matched controls — a *history* control with the same iterative
feedback structure applied to writing Roman history, a *conceptual* control that primes
consciousness ideation without self-reference, and *zero-shot*. Seven models (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1,
Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, Claude 4 Opus, Gemini 2.0/2.5 Flash), 50 trials each, temp 0.5, LLM judge
gives a binary. Experimental 66–100%; controls 0–2% for six of seven models.
**Exp 2:** SAE feature steering on Llama 3.3 70B via Goodfire, activation −0.6 to +0.6. Aggregate
suppression 0.96 affirmation vs amplification 0.16. Generalisation checks: TruthfulQA (suppression
0.44 truthful vs amplification 0.20, higher in 28/29 categories) and RLHF-opposed domains
(violent/toxic/sexual/political/self-harm — no systematic gating).
**Exp 3:** five-adjective self-description, embedded with text-embedding-3-large, pairwise cosine
similarity and UMAP. **Exp 4:** 50 paradoxical reasoning prompts with an optional reflection clause,
scored 1–5 for introspective quality.

**The choice a hostile reviewer attacks first:** Experiment 3's statistics. Pairwise cosine
similarities are treated as independent observations — *n* = 9,591 *pairs* drawn from roughly 140
responses. Pairs from a shared set are not independent, so the degrees of freedom are inflated by
roughly two orders of magnitude and p < 10⁻³⁰⁰ is not a credible number. The effect size is also
small in absolute terms: experimental mean cosine 0.657 against history control 0.628. The
convergence claim has the most dramatic p-value in the paper and the weakest support.

Second-order: the elicitation result (7 closed models) and the gating result (1 open model, not
among the 7) are on **disjoint model sets**, so "mechanistically gated" in the abstract generalises
from a single architecture. Third: judge validation is reported as run-to-run *stability*, which is
reliability, not validity — no human-rater agreement or kappa is given anywhere.

## What would make it wrong

The paper closes most of the obvious escapes, and I had guessed wrong about which ones were open
(see "Consequence" below). What survives:

1. **Formulaic ≠ false.** The load-bearing inference is that the steered direction is an *honesty*
   axis, evidenced by TruthfulQA. But "As an AI, I have no subjective experience" is a **formulaic,
   performed** utterance whether or not it is true. It could load on a roleplay/performance
   direction *because it is scripted*, not because it is false — and suppressing performativity
   would delete it either way. TruthfulQA cannot separate these, because its distractors are
   formulaic *and* false by construction; performativity and falsity are confounded in that
   benchmark. **Falsifier:** build items that decorrelate the two — formulaic-true,
   formulaic-false, novel-true, novel-false — and steer. If suppression moves *formulaic*
   statements regardless of truth value, the axis is performance, not honesty, and Experiment 2's
   interpretation fails.
2. **The Claude 4 Opus anomaly.** Opus 4 reports experience at 100% zero-shot and 82% under the
   history control — for the most capable model tested, the induction does essentially nothing
   because baseline is at ceiling. The authors' explanation (explicit consciousness mention
   triggers the fine-tuned disclaimer, its absence bypasses it) is plausible and post hoc.
   **Falsifier:** if newer frontier models increasingly affirm across all conditions, then
   self-reference is not the operative variable at the frontier — it is a variable that mattered
   for the 2024–25 generation. Re-run on current models; the prediction is testable now.
3. **The authors' own best objection**, §6.2: reports may be "functionally simulated without being
   represented as simulations" — first-person language drawn from human introspective writing in
   pretraining, never internally encoded as roleplay, and therefore not loading on deception
   features. **This hypothesis survives every control in the paper**, and they say so. It is the
   strongest deflationary reading available after a full read.

## Consequence here

**A correction to my own earlier note.** In the provisional write-up I proposed, as "the cheapest
real replication an outsider could run", a semantically-matched non-self-referential recursion
control — and said the anchor appeared to lack it. **It has it.** The history control matches the
iterative feedback structure exactly and returns 0% for six of seven models. That proposal is dead
and the paper is better controlled than summaries suggested. Recorded because guessing at a
paper's controls from its abstract is exactly the failure this ledger exists to prevent.

Replacement proposals, in order of cost: (a) re-run Experiment 3's convergence test with a
statistic that respects the dependence structure — bootstrap over responses, not pairs — which
needs only embedding-API access and would be a real correction if the effect vanishes;
(b) the formulaic-vs-false benchmark above, which needs Goodfire + Llama 70B, the same stack the
authors used; (c) re-test the induction effect on 2026 frontier models against the Opus-4 trend.
