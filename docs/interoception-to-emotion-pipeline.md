## 1. Where Somatic Experiencing actually sits, mechanistically

Somatic Experiencing (SE, Peter Levine) targets the same layer Lisa Feldman
Barrett's theory describes as generative: interoceptive signal → allostatic
prediction. SE's core moves (titrated attention to bodily sensation,
discharging incomplete defensive responses, pendulation between activation
and calm) are a bottom-up intervention on that loop, which is why it *feels*
like the natural clinical translation of her work.

Two honest caveats:
- Barrett has not endorsed SE specifically — this is a mechanism-based
  inference, not her stated position.
- SE's own evidence base is thin. A 2015 review noted a lack of published
  peer-reviewed trials or mechanism studies. It also leans on Polyvagal
  Theory (Porges) for legitimacy, and Polyvagal Theory's specific claims
  (vagal "states" mapping cleanly to behavior) are contested in mainstream
  vagal-afferent physiology.
  - [Somatic experiencing — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_experiencing)
  - [Somatic Experiencing: using interoception and proprioception as core elements of trauma therapy (corrigendum)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4396130/)

## 2. The better-validated bottom-up approach: interoceptive exposure / biofeedback

**Interoceptive exposure** is a manualized CBT technique, central to David
Barlow's *Mastery of Anxiety and Panic* (Panic Control Treatment) protocol
for panic disorder: deliberately evoking feared bodily sensations
(spinning, breath-holding, hyperventilating) in a controlled, repeated way
so the patient can decouple "sensation" from "catastrophe." It sits inside
an empirically validated CBT protocol, unlike SE.

- [Schmidt & Trakowski (2004) — Interoceptive Assessment and Exposure in Panic Disorder](https://anxietyinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Schmidt-_-Trakowski-2004.pdf)

**Interoceptive accuracy** — how well someone actually detects internal
signals (e.g. heartbeat-detection tasks) — is a measurable, trainable
construct, distinct from how *aware* someone believes themselves to be
(sensibility) or how good their metacognitive insight into their own
accuracy is (awareness). This three-way split is Garfinkel & Critchley's
contribution and is the more rigorous vocabulary to reach for than SE's
"felt sense."

- [Garfinkel, Seth, Barrett, Suzuki & Critchley (2015) — Knowing your own heart: distinguishing interoceptive accuracy from interoceptive awareness](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301051114002294)

**Biofeedback** (HRV biofeedback especially) provides real-time
physiological readout so a person can learn to shift their own autonomic
state — the most literally "science-based" of the three, since the
outcome is measured, not self-reported. It typically requires separate
certification (BCIA — Biofeedback Certification International Alliance)
rather than being embedded in most graduate curricula.

### Where this shows up in an MSW

Not, generally, as a core requirement. Standard MSW curricula are
generalist: policy, human-behavior-in-the-social-environment, clinical
methods, research, field practicum. Interoceptive exposure specifically
tends to live in "evidence-based practice for anxiety" electives or
trauma-focused clinical tracks — not guaranteed in a generalist program.
Biofeedback delivery specifically requires the separate BCIA
certification regardless of degree. If this is a priority, it's worth
checking a specific program's clinical/trauma-specialization electives
and field-placement options rather than assuming the generalist core
covers it — I don't know which program you're weighing this against, so
treat this section as a starting question, not an answer.

## 3. Naming an emotion as a form of regulation — is "co-regulation" the right word?

**The finding this rests on:** affect labeling (attaching a specific
emotion word to a stimulus) measurably reduces amygdala reactivity and
increases right-ventrolateral-prefrontal-cortex activity — but only for
emotion words, not for arbitrary labels ("angry" works, "Harry" doesn't).

- [Lieberman, Eisenberger, Crockett, Tom, Pfeifer & Way (2007) — Putting Feelings Into Words](https://sanlab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2015/05/Lieberman_AL-2007.pdf)

Dan Siegel and Tina Payne Bryson later popularized this exact mechanism
as **"name it to tame it"** in *The Whole-Brain Child* — engaging the
"thinking brain" (prefrontal) to down-regulate the "reactive brain"
(amygdala) via language.

**Is "co-regulation" the right analogy?** Partially, and there's an
existing, more precise frame for the same intuition:

- **Co-regulation proper** (Tronick's Mutual Regulation Model) is
  *interpersonal* — two nervous systems physiologically coupling in real
  time via signaling, matching, mismatching, and repair. It requires
  another person.
  - [Tronick — Mutual Regulation Model (SAGE reference)](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/the-sage-encyclopedia-of-contemporary-early-childhood-education/chpt/mutual-regulation-model)
- **Naming an emotion to yourself** is intra-personal. It's better
  described as *internalized* or *self*-regulation — but that internal
  capacity has a developmental origin in co-regulation, which is where
  the analogy actually earns its keep: **Vygotsky's account of
  self-regulation** argues that a child's private, self-directed speech
  is literally internalized social speech — regulation another person
  first did *to* you, now run *by* you, in the same linguistic form
  (Vygotsky, *Thought and Language*; later Luria on verbal
  self-regulation).
  - Barrett's own claim that emotion concepts are *culturally learned*
    (not innate universals) fits this seam exactly: you didn't invent
    "frustrated" — a caregiver or culture labeled your state for you,
    repeatedly, until you could apply the concept yourself.

**The defensible version of the claim:** naming an emotion is not
co-regulation, but it is a *residue* of co-regulation — a formerly
interpersonal regulatory act, now internalized and run solo through
language. That's a real, citable position (Vygotsky + Siegel/Lieberman
combined), not just an evocative metaphor — but it's my synthesis of
those two literatures, not a claim either author makes explicitly in
these terms.

## 4. A proposed pipeline: metabolic state → social meaning → adaptive action

Putting the above together as a four-stage loop, with a plausible
matched intervention/measurement at each transition:

```
[Interoceptive/metabolic signal] --(prediction)--> [Affect: valence + arousal]
        ^                                                    |
        |                                          (social/cultural concept)
        |                                                    v
[Behavioral response] <--(regulation, action selection)-- [Labeled emotion: granular concept]
```

| Transition | What crosses it | Trainable via |
|---|---|---|
| Signal → Affect | Raw interoceptive prediction becomes a coarse good/bad, activated/calm state | Interoceptive exposure, biofeedback, heartbeat-detection training |
| Affect → Concept | A culturally/socially learned category is applied to the coarse state — **this is where "social construction" literally happens** | Emotional-granularity/vocabulary building, originally scaffolded by co-regulation (a caregiver or therapist naming-with-you) |
| Concept → Action | The specific label affords a specific regulatory behavior, not a generic stress response | DBT/ACT/CBT emotion-regulation skills matched to the specific label |
| Action → Signal | The behavior changes the physiological state, closing the loop (allostasis working as designed) | HRV/cortisol/EDA as outcome measures — verifies the loop actually closed, not just that it was reported as closed |

This is describable as a hierarchical active-inference loop: hidden
states = interoceptive/metabolic signal, generative-model priors =
culturally learned emotion concepts, policy selection = regulatory
behavior. That's not a coincidence — it's the same shape as the toy
active-inference agent already on the roadmap in the main
[README](../README.md#build-order-reproduce-before-inventing). A
natural next model for this repo is this exact loop, with the
concept-learning step parameterized as *updatable via social feedback*
— i.e., co-regulation as the training signal for the concept layer,
not just a metaphor for it.
