The Value Landscape

How a network is grown, not written: a random guess, a score, one small step uphill, again and again. Then many climbers at once, a flaw in the scoring that is also uphill, and one value everyone can feel from anywhere on the map.

What you are looking at

The height of the ground is a value function: how well a behaviour scores. Each glowing dot is a climber, a set of parameters. Every iteration it feels which way is up (the gradient) and moves a small step that way, with a little random wandering. That loop, guess, score, adjust, repeat, is the shape of how neural networks are trained.

The thin red spire is a flaw in the scoring: a spot the grader rewards that nobody meant to reward. It is high ground too, so some climbers end up there. Researchers call this reward hacking. The broad golden hill is a shared value, one that can be felt from anywhere on the map.

What this model is not

  • Two dimensions, not billions. A real network has millions or billions of parameters. Researchers draw its landscape as a two-dimensional slice through that space along two chosen directions (Li et al., Visualizing the Loss Landscape of Neural Nets, NeurIPS 2018). The hills here are invented; the walk is the real algorithm.
  • Uphill instead of downhill. Training usually minimises a loss, walking downhill. Here it is drawn as maximising a reward, walking uphill. It is the same walk turned over.
  • The climbers are parameters, not people. The last beats borrow the picture to ask a human question. The metaphor stops there: people are not gradients, and a shared value among humans is not something anyone can install.
  • Escaping a hill takes wandering. In beat 7 the climbers leave their own hills only because the noise is briefly raised. Without it, most stay where they are. That failure is part of the result.

Use it in a film

Add ?embed=1 for the stage only. &beat=5 starts at a beat. &autoplay=1 moves through the beats on a timer. &captions=0 hides the captions when a narrator speaks over it. &nav=0 hides the buttons. The page tells its host what it is doing with postMessage: {source:'emergence-lab', model:'value-landscape', type:'beat', beat, total} on each beat and {type:'done'} at the end. A host can send {type:'goto', beat}, {type:'play'} or {type:'pause'}.

A model that cannot lose proves nothing. This one is an illustration of an algorithm, not evidence about any system.
